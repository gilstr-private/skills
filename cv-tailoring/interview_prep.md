# Interview Prep — Stock Answers and Notes

This file holds verbal answers to expected interview questions. The CV makes claims; this file ensures the conversation that follows holds them up.

## "Tell me about yourself" (the opening question)

Pattern: 90 seconds, three beats.

1. **Who I am now.** "I'm CTO at Center for Educational Technology in Tel Aviv — Israel's leading K-12 ed-tech organization, reaching over a million students. I'm the architect-strategist CTO, focused on technology direction, AI-augmented engineering, and modernizing our legacy platforms."

2. **How I got here.** "Before that, I spent 14 years at CG Solutions / Groove Technologies — joined to build a new team, was promoted to combined CTO and VP R&D about a year in, and over 13 years scaled that organization from 10 to 60 people across R&D, DevOps, IT, NOC, and Product. We built a B2B SaaS platform handling hundreds of millions of daily transactions at 99.99% availability. The part I'm proudest of is the engineering transformation — six years of focused work to reach DORA-elite performance, measured continuously."

## "Tell me about the 'ownership transition' at CG Solutions"

> Partnership and shareholding changed; the company was restructured and renamed — from Gamescale to CG Solutions, later Groove Technologies. I continued in the dual CTO and VP R&D role through the transition. New owners typically clean house at the top; they kept me. That's most of what's worth saying about it.

---

## "How did you measure DORA-elite?"

> Haystack, integrated with Jira, Jenkins, and Git — continuous instrumentation across the full delivery pipeline. We hit elite-tier on deployment frequency and MTTR. High-tier — one grade below elite — on lead time for changes and change failure rate. That profile, where you ship fast and recover fast but your planning front-end is slightly slower, is common at organizations that prioritized operational excellence. We owned it and worked on the lead-time side throughout. The transformation took six years end to end.

---

## "What were your direct reports at CG Solutions?"

> Five at peak: Head of Product for our main line of business, Head of Product for the second line of business, R&D Manager, Head of DevOps and IT, and the NOC Manager. The NOC piece is unusual at this scope — most CTOs at a 60-person org don't have 24/7 operations under them. We did, because the platform couldn't go down.

---

## "How hands-on are you, really?"

> Hands-on means something different in 2026 than it did five years ago. I work with Claude Code daily — writing specs, reviewing designs, reviewing code, pairing on hard problems with AI agents and engineers. I don't type every line, but the production output is genuinely mine in a meaningful sense. I'm in the architecture, I'm in the trade-offs, I'm in the code reviews. If you're asking whether I can defend a system design at the whiteboard, the answer is yes. If you're asking whether I'd be a Staff Engineer dressed up as a CTO, the answer is no — I'm a CTO who actually engages with the work.

---

## "What's your AI experience really?"

> Honest version: solid LLM integration experience — RAG, MCP, prompt engineering. Daily user of Claude Code. Built and deployed multi-agent code review tooling at CET. Working knowledge of agentic frameworks like LangGraph — I've used them, I understand the patterns, but I won't claim deep agent-architecture expertise. If you need someone who's spent two years on agent internals, I'm not your candidate. If you need someone who can lead an engineering organization through AI adoption while keeping their hands in the work, I am.

## "What questions do you have for us?"

Always have 3–5 ready. Vary by company; some perennial good ones:
- "What's the technical decision the team got most wrong in the last two years, and what did you learn from it?"
- "What does the first 90 days look like for whoever takes this role?"
- "If I asked the engineers on this team what they'd change about how the org works, what would they say?"
- "What's the metric you'd use to know I'm succeeding six months in?"
- "What's the part of this role you're most worried about filling?"

## Notes on stylistic delivery

- **On modesty:** Gil's track record is strong. He doesn't need to oversell. Stating facts (60 people, DORA-elite, hundreds of millions of transactions) in a flat tone is more credible than enthusiasm.
- **On the gaming industry:** Don't apologize for it. The technical achievement transfers; gaming was a great training ground for high-volume, regulated, real-money systems.
- **On the connection-points distinctiveness:** Don't say "I'm good at the connection points" — show it through the answers. The CTO who codes is different from the CTO who only sets strategy; the leader whose former colleagues hire him again is different from the leader who burns bridges.
