# Editorial Principles — Gil's CV

These principles govern every tailored CV, cover letter, and LinkedIn update. Read them before producing any content. When in doubt, default to these.

## Voice and Tone

1. **First-person in the summary; third-person in the bullets.** The base CV opens with "I build the engineering organizations that build mission-critical systems — and I stay in the code while doing it." This is intentional. Keep it. Bullets stay in clean executive register.

2. **Distinctive but dignified.** The CV should stand out without being clever for its own sake. No emoji. No marketing copy. No exclamation marks. No "Highly experienced" or "Proven track record."

3. **Specific over impressive.** Every claim should be defensible under cross-examination. Specific numbers beat impressive adjectives. Named tools beat generic ones.

## What "hands-on" means in 2026

Most CTOs claim to be hands-on. Few are. Gil is — but in the modern sense. Frame hands-on work as:
- Architecture and system design
- Specifications and spec-driven development
- Design and code review
- Daily work with Claude Code and AI-augmented engineering
- Engagement with engineers on hard technical decisions

**Do not** quantify hands-on time with percentages. "50% hands-on" invites overclaim challenges; "in the modern sense" sets the right frame.

## Distinctiveness — Themes to Preserve

These are the four claims that anchor everything. Every tailored version must serve them:

1. **Built a 60-person engineering organization from scratch over 13 years.**
2. **Drove that organization to DORA-elite engineering performance** measured continuously via Haystack.
3. **Currently embedding AI-augmented engineering culture** at Israel's leading K-12 ed-tech organization.
4. **Hands-on in the modern sense** — architecture, specs, design and code review, AI-augmented engineering.

Underlying theme: **operating at the connection points** — between domain experts and engineering teams, between strategy and execution, between executive judgment and hands-on contribution.

## Language Rules

### Always
- "DORA-elite engineering performance" (not "DORA elite" or "elite-tier DORA")
- "Measured continuously via Haystack" (the brand name stays)
- ".NET Core" (not ".NET" alone, and never "C# Core")
- "Regulated real-money gaming sector" (when referring to CG Solutions' industry)
- "Architect-strategist CTO" (when describing the CET role specifically)
- "Israel's leading K-12 educational technology organization" (when describing CET)
- "Hands-on in the modern sense" (the framing phrase)
- "Promoted to CTO & VP R&D in 2012" (the CG promotion phrasing)

### Never
- "Highly experienced" or any generic opener
- "Member of the executive team" at CET (not accurate; reports to EVP)
- "Manages a 60-person organization" at CET (not accurate; cross-functional influence)
- "Online gaming industry" (use "regulated real-money gaming sector")
- "Strangler Fig" by name (use "incremental migration patterns")
- "Principal architect" at CET (CET uses just "architect")
- "By the board" describing the CG promotion (factual but reads self-congratulatory on paper)
- "Dysfunctional" describing any prior organization
- "Groundbreaking," "revolutionary," "world-class," or similar puffery
- "50% hands-on" or any other percentage
- Any CET operational metric (transaction volume, uptime) — Gil doesn't own those levers there
- DORA claims at CET — not yet at CG-level quality
- LangGraph as a top-level skill — Gil has working knowledge only

## Length

- **Target: 1 page.** US Letter, Calibri 11, ~0.5" top/bottom margins, 0.75" sides.
- **Maximum: 1.5 pages.** Only if the target role genuinely needs more detail.
- **Never 2+ pages.** Senior CV bloat is a credibility hit.

## Visual Style

- **Section dividers:** Slate-blue (#2E5C8A) horizontal rules under uppercase section headers.
- **Role headers:** Title in bold, company name in normal weight, location and dates right-aligned in muted gray.
- **CG Solutions bullets:** Use bold lead-ins ("DORA-elite engineering performance," "Greenfield to mission-critical," "Modernization at scale") for visual rhythm.
- **CET bullets:** Plain (no bold lead-ins) — the contrast with CG signals freshness vs. depth.
- **No headshots, no graphic flourishes, no two-column layouts.**

## The DORA Claim — How to Handle It

The DORA-elite claim is the strongest single line on the CV. Protect it.

**On paper:** "DORA-elite engineering performance, reached over six years of focused transformation, measured continuously via Haystack."

**In conversation, the honest expansion:**
> Elite-tier on deployment frequency and MTTR. High-tier (one grade below elite) on lead time for changes and change failure rate. Measured continuously via Haystack, integrated across Jira, Jenkins, and Git. Six years of focused work to get there.

Both versions are honest. The CV makes the top-line claim; conversation provides the nuance.

## The Gaming Industry Question

CG Solutions was a B2B SaaS platform for the regulated real-money gaming sector. This is accurate and worth keeping on the CV because:

- It anchors the unfamiliar company name (CG Solutions / Groove Technologies isn't recognizable).
- It frames the technical challenge (real-money = high stakes; regulated = compliance + audit + reliability).
- It implies the operational pressure (regulated real-money platforms can't go down).

The "regulated" qualifier matters. Don't drop it.

**For audiences potentially uncomfortable with gaming** (some enterprise / mission-driven roles), the framing softens the industry without hiding it. Don't apologize for the industry. The technical achievement transfers; the framing controls perception.

## When tailoring to a job description

- Read the JD carefully before changing anything.
- Identify which of the four core claims the JD most rewards. Foreground that one in the summary.
- Identify 2–3 bullets that don't serve the JD; replace them with alternatives from the master document.
- Adjust the skills line to lead with terms the JD emphasizes (without inventing skills).
- Adjust the role descriptions only minimally — the structural claims are stable; only emphasis changes.
- Never invent. If the JD wants something Gil doesn't have, flag it for him rather than fabricating.

## What to escalate to Gil

- Any claim you're unsure about.
- Any term from a JD that has no good match in Gil's actual experience.
- Any framing that would require Gil to overclaim in interviews.
- Any conflict between this principles file and Gil's specific request — ask before deviating.
